Chromium Open Source Health - Review Balance

Draft: under development

This page is an early draft. Its figures, methods and conclusions haven't been reviewed and may be wrong or change without notice. Please don't cite or share them yet.

The question

Chromium has grown for two decades through code review: experts give feedback, newcomers learn to need less of it, and in time they review and own code themselves and train the next newcomers. AI tools now make changes cheaper to write than to review. This page asks whether that cycle still holds, and how the community can adapt to agentic engineering in a way that improves product quality, robustness, engineering rigour and velocity, and keeps growing its people into expert engineers.

The signals

  • Balance: how much review each CL gets for its size and directory (the review load index), and how many CLs each reviewer carries.
  • Speed: hours from being sent for review to the first substantive review, and time spent waiting in Gerrit's attention set.
  • Quality: how much a CL changes after review, review rounds, and reverts within 30 days.
  • Growth: whether people need less review as they gain tenure and know an area, and whether they grow in velocity, breadth or reviewing, compared with chance and across cohorts.
  • Carrying the project: moving from author to reviewer to OWNER, who reviews newcomers, and how many mentees become mentors.

What we see so far

Per-author velocity has crept up since 2023 as the pool of active authors shrank, and in 2026 CLs got bigger too. Review per line fell: like for like, a 2026 CL gets about a third less review than one in 2023. Waits shortened in 2025 and are back to 2023 levels, and reverts haven't risen. People who joined in 2025 land more CLs and review others sooner than earlier joiners, and draw less review, which may be their reviewers' doing as much as theirs. Approvals keep concentrating on the busiest reviewers, and fewer mid-career reviewers approve newcomers' CLs, trends that began before AI tools. No single signal here separates AI from other changes in the project.

Growth paths by career stage

For each person and half-year we measure velocity (CLs per active month), size (median lines), breadth (directories among a fixed sample of their CLs), reviewing (approvals per active month) and, since 2023, independence (needing fewer threads than peers for the same size and area). Each is taken relative to everyone's median that half-year, so tools that help everyone don't count as anyone's growth. A person is improving on a path when their two-year trend beats the 80th percentile of trends from the same people with their half-years shuffled. Chance alone puts about 20% of people on any one path.

People grow mostly by reviewing more and by needing less review, not by writing bigger CLs: size sits at chance in every window. The share improving on none of the four git paths was – in 2019–20, – in 2023–24 and – in 2025–26. Most of them have five or more years' tenure. Since AI tools arrived, the low-growth share has eased slightly, not risen.

#

Why mechanical CLs are left out

Past about 50 files, review per file collapses: CLs with 100 to 199 files got – threads per file, against – for 10 to 19 files. Repetitive diffs (the same lines repeated across files) behave the same at any size. A CL counts as mechanical with 100 or more files, or with 10 or more files of which at least 80% of changed lines are repeats. The rule doesn't look at review counts, so it can't bias the review measures it filters.

#

How it's measured

Data
Chromium's public Gerrit review data (comments, threads and NoteDb history with uploads and the attention set) from July 2023, every reviewed CL's patch sets, and chromium/src main's full git history, including Reviewed-by footers from mid-2017 and OWNERS files from 2012.
People
One person across addresses: the same username at chromium.org and google.com, old SVN-era addresses, and the Gerrit owner and git author of the same CL. Tenure counts from a person's first commit on main. Bots and service accounts are left out. No individual is named or ranked.
Substantive review
An inline reviewer comment, or a reviewer thread the author answered. A lone "LGTM" is not substantive review.
Review load index
Reviewer threads ÷ the 2023–24 mean for CLs of the same size (ten log-spaced buckets) and main 2-level directory.
Lines changed after review
The added and removed lines of the first reviewed patch set's diff against the final one's, compared as multisets, so a rebase changes nothing.
Growth
Per person, the least-squares slope of each path over the half-years of a two-year window (three or more measured), shrunk toward zero by its noise and compared with slopes from shuffled half-years. Individual slopes are never published.
AI attribution
Most AI use leaves no marker, so the before/after comparison can't separate AI from other changes in 2025–26: team growth, new hires, reorganisations, or tooling unrelated to AI.
Left out
Abandoned and open CLs; release branches; other repositories (V8, Skia, DevTools). Gerrit history before July 2023 isn't fetched, so pre-AI review measures cover only 18 months. 2026 Q4 is incomplete and is left out of the quarterly charts.
