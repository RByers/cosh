Chromium Open Source Health - Review Tone

Code review is where most of Chromium's collaboration happens, and its tone shapes how welcome contributors feel. This page tracks that tone across the whole project: how warm or harsh review comments are, and how often people express frustration with AI in review. It also recognises the people who give and receive the most praise.

Each human review comment is scored by an AI model (Claude Haiku) from −1 (hostile) through 0 (neutral, including firm technical criticism) to +1 (heartfelt gratitude). Almost all review is neutral, so averages sit just above zero; the shares of glowing and grumpy comments move more. The scores are one model's reading of tone and make mistakes, so this page never names anyone for negative tone.

            How it's measured

            Comments come from Chromium's public Gerrit code review: every comment by a human account on a change owned by a human, including the change owner's replies. Bots, AI reviewers and text an agent posted under a person's account are left out. A committer is anyone Gerrit lets vote Code-Review on Chromium changes, which only committers may. That status is as of November 2026, when the data was fetched, and is used for comments of every date. The model sees the comment, its file and up to four earlier comments in its thread, labelled only as from the change's author, a reviewer or an AI, never by name.

            The prompt was tuned against 261 comments labelled by Claude Opus and spot-checked by hand. On those it agrees with Opus's labels to within 0.05 on average (Pearson r 0.92–0.93), and puts 88–90% of comments in the same class of negative, neutral or positive. The trend uses every comment from December 2025 on, and a deterministic 5% sample of comments before that; the leaderboards and the sample comments use every comment in their weeks. Code and method: docs/sentiment.md.
